Laser Hair Removal Protocol & Safety Checklist

The protocol should be built around the clinic’s exact laser or light-based device, authorized operators, patient and skin assessment, approved parameters, eye protection, treatment records, maintenance, aftercare, and complication pathway.

Device-specific framework•Approximately 10 minutes•Updated September 7, 2026
Quick answer

A laser hair removal protocol should define device identity and intended use, operator qualifications, patient and skin assessment, contraindications and precautions, test-spot policy where applicable, approved parameter framework, wavelength-specific eye protection, room controls, treatment documentation, aftercare, adverse-event response, maintenance, training, and approval.

Start with the exact device—not “laser” in general

Laser, intense pulsed light, and other light-based systems are not interchangeable. The protocol should identify manufacturer, model, technology, wavelengths, handpieces, cooling method, software, accessories, intended use, maintenance requirements, and current instructions.

The protocol should not invent settings.

Parameters require device-specific training, manufacturer information, qualified clinical approval, treatment-area assessment, skin and hair characteristics, and individual judgment.

The complete document set

Clinical protocol

Roles, screening, preparation, device workflow, settings framework, documentation, aftercare, and escalation.

Patient forms

Intake, consent, pre-care, aftercare, photography choices, treatment plan, and follow-up.

Treatment and device records

Assessment, test spot, settings, areas, response, maintenance, calibration, service, and incident information.

Safety program

Laser safety responsibility, room controls, warning signs, eyewear, plume where applicable, fire safety, training, and emergency response.

Core clinical workflow

1

Verify operator authority and competency

Match the procedure, device, jurisdiction, license, supervision, training, and competency requirements.

2

Assess the patient and treatment area

Review history, medications, recent exposure or procedures, skin and hair characteristics, treatment goals, contraindications, precautions, and reasons to defer.

3

Prepare the room and device

Confirm controlled access, warning signage, wavelength-appropriate eye protection, device checks, cooling, surfaces, flammable materials, and emergency readiness.

4

Document treatment and response

Record areas, device, handpiece, settings, test spot if used, skin response, cooling, patient tolerance, endpoint, and any variation or stop decision.

5

Provide aftercare and follow-up

Use clinic-approved instructions covering expected response, skin care, sun precautions, concerning symptoms, contact methods, and timing of the next assessment.

Treatment-record checklist

  • Patient and operator identifiers
  • Assessment and skin classification
  • Hair and treatment-area findings
  • Consent and photography status
  • Device, handpiece, and wavelength
  • Test spot and response where applicable
  • Settings by area
  • Cooling and protective measures
  • Endpoint and patient response
  • Aftercare and follow-up plan

Laser safety is more than protective eyewear

OSHA notes that laser exposure can injure eyes and skin. Protective eyewear must be appropriate for the specific wavelength and optical density. The clinic’s program should also address controlled access, signage, reflective surfaces, equipment keys, staff and patient protection, fire risk, electrical safety, plume when applicable, maintenance, incidents, and emergency response.

Review and maintenance

Compare the protocol with current device labeling, FDA clearance information, manufacturer instructions, state requirements, operator training, maintenance records, consent, treatment record, aftercare, and complication workflow. Assign scheduled and trigger-based review dates.

Frequently asked questions

Can one protocol cover laser and IPL?

A shared framework may be possible, but each device’s technology, wavelength, indications, settings, eye protection, training, and instructions must remain distinct.

Who can perform laser hair removal?

Requirements vary substantially by state and may address operator licensing, physician supervision, facility registration, and device-specific training.

Should settings be printed in the protocol?

Use a qualified, device-specific framework with controlled supporting references; avoid copying settings from unrelated devices or clinics.
