Microneedling Protocol Documentation Checklist

A clinic-specific microneedling package should connect provider authority, patient screening, the exact device and indication, infection prevention, product use, treatment records, aftercare, complication response, and clinical approval.

Device-specific checklist•Approximately 10 minutes•Updated September 7, 2026
Quick answer

The protocol should define the service and device, permitted provider roles, evaluation and contraindication review, approved treatment areas and parameters, infection-control and cartridge practices, topical or post-procedure products, treatment-record fields, patient consent, aftercare, follow-up, complication escalation, training, and version approval.

First define what “microneedling” means in this clinic

Mechanical microneedling and radiofrequency microneedling are not interchangeable. Devices have specific indications, designs, settings, contraindications, instructions, and risks. The protocol should name the manufacturer, model, intended use, treatment areas, accessories, cartridges, and whether energy is delivered.

Do not write the protocol around a generic device.

FDA-authorized uses, manufacturer instructions, state scope rules, provider training, and individual patient assessment should govern the clinic’s approved workflow.

The complete microneedling document set

Clinical protocol

Purpose, scope, roles, screening, preparation, device workflow, documentation, aftercare, and escalation.

Orders or authorization

Any evaluation, prescription, standing order, supervision, or delegation documentation required for the state, device, products, and team.

Patient forms

Intake, procedure-specific consent, pre-care, aftercare, photography authorization where used, and follow-up guidance.

Operational records

Treatment record, room setup, cleaning, device maintenance, cartridge tracking, training, incident report, and version control.

Core protocol sections

1

Provider roles and authority

Identify who evaluates, orders, treats, supervises, provides topical medication authorization where used, and manages complications.

2

Patient screening

Organize history, medications, allergies, skin assessment, treatment goals, prior procedures, contraindications, precautions, and reasons to defer or refer.

3

Device and supplies

Name the device, software where relevant, approved cartridges and accessories, maintenance, single-use items, and manufacturer instructions.

4

Preparation and infection prevention

Connect hand hygiene, PPE, skin preparation, clean and contaminated zones, single-use components, sharps, environmental cleaning, and exposure response.

5

Approved clinical workflow

Describe treatment-area confirmation, device setup, parameter documentation, patient monitoring, endpoint assessment, and stop criteria without replacing device-specific training or judgment.

6

Aftercare and escalation

Define expected recovery, product guidance, activity and sun precautions, concerning symptoms, contact methods, follow-up, urgent referral, and reporting.

Microneedling treatment-record checklist

  • Patient and date of service
  • Evaluating and treating professionals
  • Indication and treatment areas
  • Consent and photography status
  • Skin assessment and relevant screening
  • Exact device and cartridge identifier
  • Lot or serial tracking where applicable
  • Parameters by treatment area
  • Skin-preparation method
  • Products applied before, during, or after
  • Observed endpoint and patient response
  • Aftercare, follow-up, and escalation

Document all substances used with or after the device. FDA notes that microneedling devices are not approved for delivering cosmetics, topical medications, vitamin solutions, drugs, or blood products into the skin; the clinic should carefully review intended use, evidence, product status, and applicable requirements.

RF microneedling needs a distinct pathway

Radiofrequency microneedling combines skin penetration with energy delivery and requires device-specific controls. In its October 2025 safety communication, FDA reported serious complications with certain uses, including burns, scarring, fat loss, disfigurement, and nerve damage. Clinics offering RF microneedling should separately address operator qualifications, parameter controls, treatment areas and depths, grounding or accessory requirements, skin response, stop criteria, complication recognition, and FDA reporting.

Review before implementation

Confirm the protocol matches the current FDA information, device labeling and instructions, state scope rules, clinician training, infection-prevention program, exposure-control plan, emergency resources, consent, treatment record, aftercare, and incident-reporting workflow. Final clinical approval remains with the clinic’s qualified medical director or supervising provider.

Frequently asked questions

Can one protocol cover standard and RF microneedling?

They may share some policies, but device, energy, risk, training, treatment-record, and complication requirements should remain clearly distinguished.

Should the template include exact depths and settings?

Clinical parameters should be device-, indication-, treatment-area-, and patient-specific, and approved by qualified clinical leadership using current instructions and training.

Can PRP or serums be added to the protocol?

Any product used with microneedling requires separate review of intended use, product status, evidence, infection risk, authorization, consent, and documentation. It should not be silently added to a generic protocol.

Who can perform microneedling?

That depends on the state, depth, device, energy, medications or products used, professional license, supervision, and other facts. Obtain state-specific clinical and legal review.
